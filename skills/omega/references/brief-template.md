# Brief and handoff

Use the user's language. Scale detail to the job. For a simple image, a short paragraph and a few constraints may be enough; for a substantial application, keep the following sections as needed.

## Goal

State the intended result, audience, and use. Preserve important user phrasing.

## Confirmed requirements

Record essential behaviors or visible characteristics, priorities, constraints, available resources, and explicit exclusions. Only label a point confirmed when supported by the user's words or relevant authorized context.

## Proposed approach

Translate intent into creative or technical choices. Explain consequential choices in plain language. Distinguish recommendations from user-selected requirements. Include accessibility, privacy, operational, or delivery considerations when relevant.

## Assumptions and open points

For each material unknown, state the provisional assumption, practical consequence, and whether it blocks the next step. Do not include irrelevant unknowns for completeness.

## Acceptance checks

Specify observable checks tied to the request. For example:

- Image: subject and requested weather remain identifiable at the intended display size; text has the requested clear area.
- Application: a permitted user can complete the primary workflow; an unauthorized user cannot retrieve another user's private records; recovery behavior is verified where required.
- Writing: essential supplied facts are preserved; the intended action is explicit; unsupported claims are absent.

Do not promise every check is automated or already passed. Identify judgment-based checks honestly.

## Next deliverable

Name the next requested output: reusable prompt, concept comparison, prototype, specification, or finished artifact. Preserve original scope and existing authorization. Separate prototype assumptions from production readiness.
